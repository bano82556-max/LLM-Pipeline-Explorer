First-Year BTech Quick Summary: Why Does LLM Tokenization & Embedding Work?

1. Fixed Vocabulary

An LLM cannot have a unique number for all billions of words. It uses a fixed vocabulary of ~30,000 to 100,000 tokens.

2. Subword Power

By breaking rare words into common fragments (##s, ##ing), the model can process any word without encountering unknown tokens.

3. Vector Space

Embeddings represent meaning as geometric coordinates. Synonyms and related topics point in nearby directions in vector space.

4. Cosine Angles

Instead of measuring distance in characters, we measure the cosine of the angle between vectors: 1.0 means parallel meaning, 0.0 means unrelated!

First-Year BTech Concept Guide

Welcome to the inner workings of Large Language Models!

When you type a prompt into ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini, the model doesn't see English letters the way humans do. Here is the exact journey your words take:

Step 1: Subword Tokenization

Language models look up words in an internal dictionary. But languages have millions of words! If every word needed its own number, the model's vocabulary would be too large to fit in GPU memory. Subword tokenization solves this by breaking rare or compound words into small building blocks:

"dogs" → ["dog", "##s"]
"jumping" → ["jump", "##ing"]
"unbelievable" → ["un", "##believ", "##able"]
Step 2: Vector Embeddings

Each token ID is converted into a vector (a list of numbers, e.g. 768 dimensions in BERT or 4,096 in LLaMA). You can think of each number as measuring a specific concept, such as "How much is this about animals?" or "Is this a computer science term?"

Step 3: Dimensionality Reduction (PCA)

Humans cannot visualize 24 or 768 dimensions. Principal Component Analysis (PCA) calculates the 3 directions of greatest variation and projects the points onto our 3D interactive plot without losing the underlying cluster patterns!

Step 4: Cosine Similarity

To compare how similar two sentences are, we calculate the angle between their vector arrows. When the angle is $0^\circ$, $\cos(0) = 1.0$ (identical meaning). When they are perpendicular ($90^\circ$), $\cos(90) = 0.0$ (completely unrelated).
